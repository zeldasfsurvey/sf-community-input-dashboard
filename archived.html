4 · Knowledge Bearers & high-priority items

Most-emphasised entries

Two signals lift an entry to the top of the list: it was raised by more than one community member (a high-mention item), or our team flagged the source as a Knowledge Bearer / Partner. Sort the table by any column.

What is a Knowledge Bearer? A Knowledge Bearer or Partner is a community member, organisation, or institution our team identified as a primary holder of cultural memory, history, or expertise — someone whose continued participation is essential for accurate representation in the Historic Context Statements.

5 · What events generated the most input?

Outreach moments, in chronological order

Each bar is one event, sorted by date. The bar's height shows how many entries that event produced; the colour segments show which cultural communities those entries belonged to. Tall bars are the most productive outreach moments; the colour mix shows which communities our team reached when.

6 · Gaps and questions raised

Things flagged as missing, lost, or at risk

These entries suggest community members identified something as lost, at risk, or not yet recognised. The list is built by scanning team notes for words and phrases like "missing", "lost", "demolished", "displaced", "no longer", "should be", "forgotten", "overlooked". Each entry is grouped by the cultural community that raised it.

These flags are the community's signal of cultural loss and risk. They deserve a closer look from the survey team and may inform recommendations in the final Historic Context Statements.

This dashboard was generated from Community_Input.csv (raw entries) and Community_Input_Dashboard_-_Global.csv (synthesised themes) collected by the SF Planning Department survey team during HCS outreach. Internal team notes are not displayed publicly except as short, redacted snippets in section 6 to surface community-flagged gaps.
